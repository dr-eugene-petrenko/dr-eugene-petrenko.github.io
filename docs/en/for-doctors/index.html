For doctors: what has changed in practice

Brief practical changes from new 2025–2026 guidelines for obstetrician-gynecologists, residents and colleagues in related specialties. Before applying them, check the full text of the guideline and the current regulations of the Ministry of Health of Ukraine.

🩸 Postpartum haemorrhage

WHO / FIGO / ICM consolidated guidelines2025

  • New objective criteria for diagnosing PPH and an early response
  • Measuring blood loss with calibrated collection drapes
  • First-response bundle MOTIVE: uterine massage, uterotonics, tranexamic acid, IV fluids, examination of the genital tract, escalation of care — all at once
  • Anaemia prevention: daily iron + folic acid, intravenous iron when rapid correction is needed
Source: WHO, 2025

🤰 Induction and management of labour

ACOG CPG No. 9 — cervical ripening2025

  • Pharmacological, mechanical or combined methods are recommended
  • Oral misoprostol 20–25 mcg every 2 h or vaginal 25 mcg every 3–6 h
  • A balloon catheter combined with misoprostol shortens the time to birth
  • Outpatient cervical ripening is an option for low-risk patients
  • Prostaglandins are contraindicated after caesarean section or uterine surgery
Source: ACOG, Obstet Gynecol 2025;146:148–160

ACOG CPG No. 8 — first and second stages of labour2024

  • Active phase — from 6 cm dilatation
  • Prolonged second stage: more than 3 h of pushing in nulliparous and more than 2 h in multiparous women
  • Pushing — from full dilatation; operative vaginal birth is an alternative to caesarean section for second-stage arrest
Source: ACOG, 2024

ACOG CPG No. 10 — intrapartum fetal heart rate monitoring2025

  • Routine maternal oxygen is not recommended for category II–III tracings without hypoxaemia
  • Category III not responding to intrauterine resuscitation — expedited delivery
  • Routine STAN, fetal pulse oximetry or reliance on computerised interpretation alone are not recommended
Source: ACOG, Obstet Gynecol 2025;146:583–599

🏥 Placenta and caesarean section

RCOG Green-top No. 27a — placenta praevia and PAS2026

  • Placenta accreta spectrum can occur not only with placenta praevia or a previous caesarean scar
  • Intraoperative cell salvage is considered safe and beneficial
Source: RCOG, 2026

My position. Caesarean section without medical indication, at the woman's request, should be limited. Modern caesarean section is safe, but "everything is a medicine, everything is a poison": the art of obstetrics is to keep weighing risks, not to create additional ones.

❤️ Medical conditions in pregnancy

ESC — cardiovascular disease and pregnancy2025

  • Pregnancy Heart Team — mandatory for women at mWHO 2.0 class II–III risk and above
  • Updated mWHO 2.0 risk stratification, genetic counselling for inherited cardiovascular disease
  • Target blood pressure in pregnancy — below 140/90 mmHg
Source: ESC, 2025

ATA — thyroid disease before, during and after pregnancy2026

  • TPO antibody status no longer determines the decision on levothyroxine in subclinical hypothyroidism
  • Updated risk factors as indications for thyroid function testing
Source: ATA, Thyroid 2026; see also RCOG Green-top No. 76 (2025)

🌸 Gynecology and reproduction

ACOG CPG No. 11 — diagnosis of endometriosis2026

  • A clinical (presumptive) diagnosis based on history, symptoms and examination is sufficient to start treatment
  • Diagnostic laparoscopy is no longer a prerequisite
  • The recommendations also apply to adolescents; blood/urine biomarkers are not recommended
Source: ACOG, 2026

Infertility and contraception2025–2026

  • WHO: the first global infertility guideline — 40 recommendations, a stepwise approach from counselling to ART, psychosocial support
  • NICE NG257: updated assessment and treatment of fertility problems, a separate pathway for endometriosis
  • WHO MEC, 6th edition: updated medical eligibility criteria for contraceptive use
Source: WHO 2025 · NICE 2026 · WHO MEC 2025

🩺 Pre-eclampsia and hypertension

Aspirin for pre-eclampsia prevention2019–2025

  • First-trimester screening by risk factors, MAP, PlGF and uterine artery PI (FIGO, ISSHP)
  • High risk: ~150 mg aspirin at night from 11–14+6 wks to 36 wks — in the ASPRE trial, preterm pre-eclampsia fell by 62%
  • ACOG/USPSTF: 81 mg from 12 wks (ideally before 16) until birth, based on clinical risk factors
Source: FIGO, 2019 · ASPRE, NEJM 2017

Chronic hypertension: treat from 140/902022

  • CHAP: antihypertensive treatment targeting BP <140/90 reduced severe pre-eclampsia, preterm birth before 35 wks, placental abruption and perinatal loss
  • No increase in small-for-gestational-age newborns was found
  • ACOG endorsed the 140/90 threshold for starting or intensifying treatment
Source: CHAP, NEJM 2022

🍬 Diabetes and pregnancy

Screening for gestational and pregestational diabetes2024

  • Routine GDM screening before 24 wks is not recommended — benefit of early treatment is not proven
  • With risk factors — screen for pregestational diabetes at the first visit
  • Everyone else — screen at 24–28 wks; after GDM — a diabetes test 4–12 wks after birth
  • If diet is not enough, insulin remains the first-line medicine
Source: ACOG Clinical Practice Update, 2024

💉 Vaccination in pregnancy

RSV, pertussis, influenza2023–2025

  • RSVpreF (Abrysvo): a single dose at 32+0–36+6 wks protects the newborn from severe RSV infection in the first 6 months; alternative — a monoclonal antibody for the newborn
  • Tdap: at 27–36 wks in every pregnancy
  • Influenza: at any stage of pregnancy during the season
Source: CDC / ACOG

⏳ Preterm birth

Progesterone: what remains in the evidence2021–2023

  • Vaginal progesterone in singleton pregnancy with a short cervix (≤25 mm) reduces the risk of preterm birth (EPPPIC individual participant data meta-analysis)
  • 17-OHPC (Makena) was withdrawn by the FDA in 2023: the PROLONG trial did not confirm efficacy
Source: EPPPIC, Lancet 2021 · FDA, 2023

🔬 Cervical screening, menopause, PMOS (PCOS)

HPV test is the primary method, self-sampling allowed2025–2026

  • 30–65 years: primary HPV testing every 5 years is the preferred strategy; a self-collected vaginal sample is acceptable
  • ACS: after a negative self-collected test — repeat in 3 years, after clinician collection — in 5
  • 21–29 years: cytology every 3 years
  • WHO: a single dose of HPV vaccine is sufficient at 9–20 years
Source: HHS/HRSA, JAMA 2026 · ACS, 2025 · WHO, 2022

Menopause: new non-hormonal medicines2023–2025

  • MHT remains the most effective treatment for hot flushes; for most women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, the benefits outweigh the risks
  • Fezolinetant (NK3 antagonist, FDA 2023) and elinzanetant (NK1/NK3 antagonist, FDA October 2025) — an option when MHT is contraindicated or not wanted
Source: The Menopause Society, 2022 · OASIS 1–2, JAMA 2024

PMOS — the new name for PCOS: what has changed2026

  • Since 12 May 2026 polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) has been renamed polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS) — a global consensus, The Lancet; announced at the European Congress of Endocrinology
  • Reason: the "cysts" are arrested follicles, and the syndrome itself is a systemic endocrine-metabolic disorder, not only an ovarian one
  • The diagnostic criteria have not changed (≥2 of 3: oligo-/anovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovarian morphology or raised AMH — after excluding other causes)
  • Transition period — 3 years; full implementation in the 2028 update of the international guideline. Until then, "PMOS (PCOS)" is appropriate in documents
  • AMH can be used instead of ultrasound to assess polycystic ovarian morphology in adults
  • Letrozole — first-line ovulation induction
  • Mandatory screening for cardiometabolic risks, depression and anxiety
Source: International PCOS Guideline, 2023; The Lancet, 2026 (consensus on the new name)

🌱 Preconception care

Folic acid and the preconception plan2023

  • 0.4–0.8 mg folic acid daily — from at least 1 month before conception and during the first 2–3 months of pregnancy (USPSTF, grade A)
  • Higher doses — only for individual high risk of neural tube defects
  • Before pregnancy: update vaccinations (measles, rubella, chickenpox), optimise chronic conditions (HbA1c, BP, thyroid function)
Source: USPSTF, JAMA 2023

🤱 Postpartum period

Perinatal depression2023

  • Screening with a validated tool (EPDS or PHQ-9) — at least once during pregnancy and after birth
  • Zuranolone — the first oral medicine for postpartum depression (FDA, 2023), a 14-day course
Source: ACOG CPG No. 4, 2023 · FDA, 2023

Infant feeding and birth spacingWHO

  • Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then alongside solid foods until 2 years and beyond
  • The next pregnancy should ideally be planned at least 24 months after birth
Source: WHO
