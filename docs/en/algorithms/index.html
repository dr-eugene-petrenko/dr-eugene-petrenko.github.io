Emergency algorithms

Flowcharts for urgent situations in obstetrics and gynecology — for doctors, residents and midwives. Short, step by step, with doses and sources.

⚠️ Teaching aids based on international guidelines. In clinical practice, follow the current protocols of the Ministry of Health of Ukraine and of your facility.

🩸 Postpartum haemorrhage

Immediate

Recognise

Blood loss ≥300 ml + abnormal vital signs (HR↑, BP↓, shock index ≥1) or ≥500 ml. Measure blood loss objectively (calibrated drape/weighing).

↓
Immediate

Call for help

Obstetrician, anaesthetist, midwife, laboratory/blood bank. Record the time.

↓
Action

MOTIVE bundle — all at once

M — uterine massage · O — oxytocin 10 IU IM or slowly IV, then an infusion · T — tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min (within the first 3 h; repeat 1 g after 30 min if needed) · IV — 2 IV lines, crystalloids · E — examination: tone, tissue, trauma, thrombin

↓
◆ Has the bleeding stopped?
YesMonitoring for 24 h, anaemia prevention
NoEscalate ↓
↓
Action

Second-line uterotonics

Ergometrine 0.2 mg IM (not with hypertension) · carboprost 250 mcg IM every 15 min, up to 8 doses (not with asthma) · misoprostol 800 mcg sublingually

↓
Action

Mechanical methods

Bimanual compression, intrauterine balloon tamponade, aortic compression as a temporary measure

↓
Immediate

Surgery and transfusion

Massive transfusion protocol (RBC:FFP 1:1, platelets, fibrinogen) · compression sutures, devascularisation, embolisation, hysterectomy

Sources: WHO/FIGO/ICM 2025 (E-MOTIVE) · WOMAN trial
2

🩺 Severe hypertension and pre-eclampsia

Immediate

Confirm

SBP ≥160 and/or DBP ≥110 mmHg, repeated after 15 min → hypertensive emergency

↓
Action

Lower BP within 30–60 min

Labetalol 20 mg IV over 2 min → 40 → 80 mg every 10 min (max. 300 mg) · or immediate-release nifedipine 10–20 mg orally, repeat after 20 min · or hydralazine 5–10 mg IV. Target 140–150/90–100

↓
Action

Magnesium sulphate

Loading dose 4–6 g IV over 15–20 min → 1–2 g/h for at least 24 h after birth. Monitor: knee reflexes, RR ≥12/min, urine output ≥30 ml/h. Antidote: calcium gluconate 1 g IV over 10 min

↓
Assessment

Investigations

FBC, platelets, ALT/AST, creatinine, LDH, urine protein/creatinine; CTG, fetal ultrasound. HELLP? Neurological symptoms?

↓
◆ Is the condition stabilised?
Yes≥34 wks — delivery; <34 wks — corticosteroids, decision at a perinatal center
NoDelivery after maternal stabilisation
↓
Caution

Fluid restriction

Total volume ~80 ml/h — risk of pulmonary oedema

Sources: ACOG 2020/2022 · NICE NG133 · ISSHP 2021
3

⚡ Eclampsia (seizures)

Immediate

Safety and ABC

Call for help, place in left lateral position, protect from injury, oxygen, airway. Do not try to stop the seizure mechanically

↓
Action

Magnesium sulphate

4 g IV over 5–10 min (or 4–6 g), then 1–2 g/h. Recurrent seizure — another 2 g IV bolus

↓
Action

BP control

Follow the severe hypertension algorithm — target <160/110

↓
◆ Seizures continue despite MgSO₄?
YesDiazepam/midazolam, intubation, head CT/MRI
NoStabilise → deliver
↓
Caution

Delivery

Only after maternal stabilisation; fetal bradycardia during a seizure usually resolves within 3–5 min

Sources: RCOG · ACOG · WHO
4

👶 Shoulder dystocia

Immediate

Recognise

"Turtle sign", the head does not restitute, the shoulders are not delivered with routine traction. Record the time of delivery of the head

↓
Immediate

Call for help

Experienced obstetrician, neonatologist, anaesthetist. Stop maternal pushing. No fundal pressure, no forceful traction

↓
Action

First line (~90% success)

McRoberts manoeuvre (thighs to abdomen) + suprapubic pressure (on the posterior aspect of the anterior shoulder)

↓
◆ Shoulders delivered?
YesNewborn assessment, PPH prevention, documentation
NoInternal manoeuvres ↓
↓
Action

Second line

Delivery of the posterior arm · rotational manoeuvres (Rubin II, Woods) · all-fours position (Gaskin). Episiotomy — only to gain access

↓
Immediate

Third line

Cleidotomy, Zavanelli manoeuvre, symphysiotomy — in exceptional cases

Sources: RCOG Green-top No. 42 · HELPERR
5

〰️ Umbilical cord prolapse

Immediate

Call for help

Declare an emergency, theatre and neonatologist on standby

↓
Action

Relieve compression

Minimal handling of the cord · elevate the presenting part manually or fill the bladder with 500–750 ml · knee-chest position or left lateral with Trendelenburg

↓
Assessment

If needed

Tocolysis (terbutaline 0.25 mg SC) for bradycardia while preparing

↓
◆ Fully dilated and rapid vaginal delivery possible?
YesVaginal/instrumental delivery
NoCategory 1 caesarean section (target ≤30 min)
↓
Caution

Neonatologist

Present at the birth; cord blood gases

Sources: RCOG Green-top No. 50
6

❤️ Cardiac arrest in pregnancy

Immediate

Start CPR

Call the obstetric + neonatal + resuscitation teams. Compressions 30:2, high quality, in the usual position

↓
Action

Left uterine displacement

Manual — if the fundus is at or above the umbilicus (~≥20 wks)

↓
Action

ALS as in non-pregnant patients

Defibrillation — standard energies; adrenaline — standard doses; IV access above the diaphragm; early intubation by an experienced clinician

↓
◆ Return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) within 4 min?
YesPost-resuscitation care, identify the cause
NoPerimortem caesarean section — deliver by the 5th minute
↓
Caution

Look for the cause

4H/4T + obstetric: haemorrhage, magnesium toxicity, amniotic fluid embolism, cardiomyopathy, pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, sepsis, PE

Sources: AHA 2020/2023 · Resuscitation Council UK
7

🦠 Maternal sepsis (pregnancy and postpartum)

Immediate

Suspect

Modified Early Obstetric Warning Score (MEOWS): fever or hypothermia, tachycardia, tachypnoea, hypotension, altered consciousness

↓
Action

"Sepsis Six" — within 1 hour

Oxygen (SpO₂ 94–98%) · blood cultures before antibiotics · IV broad-spectrum antibiotics · lactate and FBC · IV crystalloids (500 ml bolus with reassessment) · hourly urine output

↓
Assessment

Find the source

Endometritis, chorioamnionitis, wound, urinary tract, breast, pneumonia; retained products of conception → evacuation

↓
◆ Hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/l despite fluids?
YesTransfer to ICU, vasopressors
NoMonitoring, reassessment after 6 h
↓
Caution

Fetus

Fetal condition improves as the mother is stabilised; delivery — for obstetric indications

Sources: RCOG Green-top No. 64a/b · Surviving Sepsis Campaign
8

📍 Ectopic pregnancy

◆ Haemodynamically unstable?
YesResuscitation, immediate surgery (laparoscopy/laparotomy)
NoTVUS + serum hCG ↓
↓
Assessment

Stable patient

Confirm location, measure the mass, presence of a heartbeat, free fluid

↓
Action

Methotrexate — first line if:

no significant pain · mass <35 mm without a heartbeat · hCG <1500 IU/l (possibly up to 5000) · patient able to attend follow-up

↓
Action

Surgery if:

significant pain, mass ≥35 mm, fetal heartbeat or hCG ≥5000 IU/l. Salpingectomy if the contralateral tube is healthy

↓
Caution

Rh-negative

Anti-D immunoglobulin with surgical management

Sources: NICE NG126 (2019, updated 2023) · RCOG/ESHRE
9

🫁 Suspected PE / DVT

Immediate

Don't wait for the diagnosis

Start weight-based therapeutic LMWH (if no contraindications) until VTE is excluded

↓
Assessment

Investigations

ECG, chest X-ray, oxygen saturation. Do not use D-dimer to exclude VTE in pregnancy

↓
◆ Symptoms of DVT?
YesLeg vein compression ultrasound: confirmed → treat as VTE
NoV/Q scan or CT pulmonary angiography
↓
Immediate

Massive PE with shock

Multidisciplinary team; systemic thrombolysis, surgical/catheter thrombectomy

↓
Caution

Birth

Plan interruption of LMWH and regional anaesthesia with the anaesthetist

Sources: RCOG Green-top No. 37b · ESC 2019
