🥗 Diet

  • Not "for two": about +340 kcal a day in the 2nd trimester, about +450 kcal in the 3rd
  • Fish 2–3 times a week — but not shark, swordfish or king mackerel (mercury)
  • Avoid raw meat, fish and eggs, unpasteurised milk and soft cheeses (listeria)
  • Caffeine — up to 200 mg a day (1–2 cups of coffee)

⚖️ How much weight to gain

  • BMI under 18.5: 12.5–18 kg
  • BMI 18.5–24.9: 11.5–16 kg
  • BMI 25–29.9: 7–11.5 kg
  • BMI 30 and over: 5–9 kg

🏃‍♀️ Exercise

  • 150 minutes of moderate activity a week: walking, swimming, pregnancy yoga
  • Avoid contact sports, scuba diving and activities with a risk of falling
  • After the 1st trimester, avoid long workouts lying on your back

😴 Sleep and rest

  • After 28 weeks, go to sleep on your side — it is safer for the baby
  • A pillow between your knees helps with back pain

💊 Medicines

  • Take any medicine only after talking to your doctor
  • For pain and fever — paracetamol for a short course
  • Do not use ibuprofen and similar NSAIDs after 20 weeks unless prescribed

✈️ Travel

  • In the car — wear a seat belt: the lap part below the bump, across the hips
  • Flying — usually up to 36 weeks; on long flights drink water, walk around and do leg exercises

🍷 Better to avoid

  • Alcohol — there is no safe amount in pregnancy
  • Smoking and vaping harm the placenta and the baby's growth
  • Overheating (sauna, hot bath) — especially in the 1st trimester

💞 Sex

  • Safe in an uncomplicated pregnancy
  • Restrictions apply with bleeding, placenta praevia or threatened preterm labour (on your doctor's advice)

⚠️ Seek care immediately if you have:

  • bleeding or leaking fluid
  • your baby is moving less than usual (from 28 weeks) — don't wait until morning
  • severe headache, spots in front of the eyes, swelling of the face or hands
  • severe abdominal pain, fever, chills
  • regular contractions before 37 weeks

👶 Birth and the first weeks after

⏰ When to go to the maternity hospital

  • Regular contractions: every 5 minutes, lasting about 1 minute, for an hour (first birth)
  • Your waters have broken — even without contractions
  • Bleeding or reduced baby movements

💉 Pain relief

  • Modern epidural analgesia is the most effective method and does not increase the risk of caesarean section
  • Movement, upright positions, a shower, breathing and partner support also help

🤱 The first minutes

  • Skin-to-skin contact immediately after birth
  • Delayed cord clamping — at least 1 minute
  • First breastfeed — within the first hour

🩺 After birth

  • Bleeding (lochia) lasts up to 6 weeks and gradually becomes lighter
  • First contact with a doctor — within 3 weeks, full check-up — by 12 weeks
  • Pelvic floor exercises reduce the risk of incontinence

💛 Mood

  • Tearfulness in the first 2 weeks is common — this is the "baby blues"
  • If sadness lasts longer and you have no energy or joy, it may be depression. It is treatable — tell your doctor

🛡️ Contraception

  • Ovulation may return even before your first period
  • It is better to plan the next pregnancy no sooner than 2 years later
  • We discuss the method before you leave hospital

🌸 Women's health

🔄 A normal cycle

  • Cycle — 24–38 days, period — up to 8 days
  • See a doctor if: a pad lasts less than an hour, you pass clots, or you bleed between periods or after sex

😣 Pain is not normal

  • If period pain gets in the way of study or work, endometriosis is possible
  • Today it is diagnosed without surgery: based on symptoms, examination and ultrasound

🔬 Screening by age

  • 21–29 years: cytology every 3 years
  • 30–65: HPV test every 5 years
  • Mammography: every 2 years from age 40
  • Bone density scan (osteoporosis): from age 65, earlier if there are risk factors

💉 HPV vaccine

  • Best at 9–14 years, catch-up — until 26
  • Protects against cervical cancer and other HPV-related cancers

🛡️ Contraception

  • The most reliable are the intrauterine device and the implant (fewer than 1 pregnancy per 100 women a year)
  • Emergency: a copper IUD within 5 days is the most effective; levonorgestrel pills — within 72 h, ulipristal — within 120 h

🧪 Infections

  • Condoms protect against STIs
  • Women under 25 — a yearly chlamydia and gonorrhoea test

🌗 PMOS (formerly PCOS)

  • Since May 2026, "polycystic ovary syndrome" has a new name — polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS). There are no true cysts, and the syndrome affects hormones and metabolism throughout the body
  • Irregular cycles, acne, excess hair growth — reasons to see a doctor
  • A healthy weight and exercise are the basis of treatment; effective medicines are available
  • It is worth checking blood sugar, lipids, blood pressure and mood — this is part of looking after yourself

🌡️ Menopause

  • Average age — about 51
  • Hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for hot flushes; non-hormonal medicines are also available
  • Important: bones, heart, pelvic floor

💪 Healthy lifestyle

Following WHO recommendations.

🏃‍♀️ Exercise

  • 150–300 min of moderate or 75–150 min of vigorous activity a week
  • + strength exercises twice a week

🥦 Food

  • Vegetables and fruit — at least 400 g a day
  • Sugar — less than 10% of calories, salt — less than 5 g (1 teaspoon)

😴 Sleep

  • 7–9 hours
  • A regular routine, no screens for an hour before bed

🚭 Habits

  • Smoking and vaping — quitting at any age brings benefits
  • Alcohol — the less, the better

📏 Weight

  • Waist circumference ≥80 cm — the risk for the heart and metabolism increases
  • Even 5–10% weight loss improves the cycle, blood pressure and blood sugar

🧠 Mental health

  • Stress, anxiety, insomnia — reasons to seek help, not to endure
  • Support from loved ones and a professional is a strength, not a weakness

❓ Myths and facts

Tap to see the answer.

"A pregnant woman should eat for two"

Myth. Extra calories are needed only from the 2nd trimester — about one small snack.

"No coffee during pregnancy"

Myth. Up to 200 mg of caffeine a day (1–2 cups) is acceptable.

"Don't raise your arms — the cord will wrap around the baby"

Myth. The position of the mother's arms does not affect the umbilical cord.

"Once a caesarean, always a caesarean"

Not always. Vaginal birth after caesarean succeeds in 60–80% of women if there are no contraindications.

"Ultrasound is harmful to the baby"

Myth. Diagnostic ultrasound performed for a medical reason is safe.

"You can't get pregnant while breastfeeding"

Myth. Ovulation can return even before your first period.

"Vaccines during pregnancy are dangerous"

Myth. Flu, whooping cough and RSV vaccines are recommended — they protect both mother and baby.

"An epidural causes back pain later"

Myth. Epidural analgesia does not increase the risk of chronic back pain.

"Exercise is forbidden during pregnancy"

Myth. Moderate activity lowers the risk of gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia and excessive weight gain.

"Severe period pain is normal"

Myth. Pain that disrupts your life needs to be investigated.
